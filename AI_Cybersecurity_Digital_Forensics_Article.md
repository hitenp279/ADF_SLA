# Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Cybersecurity and Digital Forensics

## Introduction

Digital systems are part of almost every area of modern life, from online banking to healthcare and business. As technology use increases, cyberattacks such as phishing, ransomware, identity theft, and data breaches also become more common. Security teams must examine large amounts of digital information to understand what happened during an incident. Artificial Intelligence (AI) can help by finding patterns, identifying unusual behaviour, and supporting investigators. Used carefully, AI can improve cybersecurity and digital forensics.

## Understanding AI in Cybersecurity

Artificial Intelligence refers to computer systems that perform tasks usually associated with human intelligence. In cybersecurity, AI techniques can analyse network traffic, system logs, emails, files, and user activity. Machine learning, a branch of AI, allows a system to learn patterns from data and use them to identify possible threats.

Traditional security tools often depend on known rules or signatures. They can detect familiar threats, but new or modified attacks may avoid detection. AI-based tools can identify unusual patterns that do not match normal activity. However, AI is not a replacement for security professionals. Legitimate activity can appear suspicious, and attackers may try to mislead AI systems, so results need human review.

## Major Applications

### 1. Detecting Malware and Ransomware

Malware is software designed to damage systems, steal information, or gain unauthorised access. AI models can examine file characteristics, code patterns, system calls, and behaviour to identify potentially harmful software, including some threats that do not match known signatures.

For example, if a program suddenly encrypts many files, changes security settings, and contacts an unfamiliar server, an AI-supported system may flag it for investigation. The alert is not proof of an attack, but it gives analysts a useful starting point.

### 2. Identifying Phishing and Online Fraud

Phishing messages try to trick people into revealing passwords, financial information, or other sensitive data. AI tools can inspect email wording, sender details, links, attachments, and message patterns to identify possible phishing attempts. They can also help detect unusual login behaviour, such as repeated failed attempts followed by access from an unfamiliar location.

These systems should support, not replace, user awareness and standard security controls. People should verify unexpected requests, avoid suspicious attachments, and use multi-factor authentication where possible.

### 3. Analysing Network Traffic

Organisations generate large volumes of network data, making manual review difficult. AI can learn typical communication patterns and flag unusual activity, such as unexpected data transfers, repeated connections to suspicious destinations, or communication at unusual times.

This helps security teams prioritise alerts and investigate possible intrusions sooner. However, sudden changes in normal business activity can also produce false alarms, and model performance depends on data quality and regular evaluation.

### 4. Supporting Digital Forensic Investigations

Digital forensics involves collecting, preserving, examining, and reporting digital evidence while maintaining its integrity. Investigators may examine computers, mobile devices, cloud services, application logs, and network records.

AI can group related files, search large document collections, identify suspicious timelines, and highlight unusual events. After a suspected account compromise, for example, an AI-assisted tool might help correlate login records, file-access events, and network activity to suggest a possible sequence of events.

AI-generated findings are leads, not proof. Investigators must verify them against original evidence and document how conclusions were reached.

## The Importance of Evidence Integrity

Digital evidence may be used in disciplinary, civil, or criminal proceedings, so it must be handled carefully. Investigators should preserve original data, work from verified forensic copies where appropriate, calculate cryptographic hashes, maintain a chain of custody, and record examination steps.

AI tools must not silently modify evidence or replace original records with summaries. Investigators should record the tool and version used, input data, relevant settings, and limitations. Human review is essential, especially when findings may affect a person's rights or reputation.

## Challenges and Ethical Concerns

AI introduces several challenges. **False positives** can cause harmless activity to be treated as an attack, while **false negatives** can allow real threats to go unnoticed. Models may also inherit weaknesses from incomplete training data. Attackers may change their behaviour to evade detection or deliberately manipulate AI systems.

Privacy is another concern. Forensic datasets can contain personal messages, photographs, financial records, and confidential business information. Investigators should access only data relevant to an authorised investigation, follow applicable laws and organisational policies, and protect sensitive results.

Some AI models are difficult to explain. A forensic report should not simply state that “the AI identified this as malicious.” It should describe the supporting evidence and explain how the conclusion was checked. Transparent methods and independent verification make findings more trustworthy.

## Best Practices for Responsible Use

Organisations and investigators can use AI more responsibly by following these practices:

- **Preserve original evidence:** Keep source data intact and verify forensic copies using appropriate hashing methods.
- **Maintain an audit trail:** Record actions, timestamps, tools, model versions, and important settings.
- **Validate results:** Compare AI findings with logs, file metadata, network records, and other evidence.
- **Use human oversight:** Let trained investigators make the final assessment and explain conclusions.
- **Protect privacy:** Limit access to authorised data and secure evidence throughout the investigation.
- **Test tools regularly:** Measure accuracy, investigate false alerts, and reassess performance as threats change.

## Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence is becoming a valuable support tool in cybersecurity and advanced digital forensics. It can help detect suspicious behaviour, examine large datasets, connect related events, and reduce the time needed to find useful leads. At the same time, AI can make mistakes, produce biased results, and create privacy concerns.

The most reliable approach combines AI's ability to process information at scale with the judgement and accountability of trained professionals. In digital forensics, evidence integrity, documented methods, and independent verification must remain central. AI should help investigators ask better questions and work more efficiently—not replace evidence or human responsibility.
